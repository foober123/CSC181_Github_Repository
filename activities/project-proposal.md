### Selected problem

A regular customer comes to get their car checked and the mechanic who has worked on it is absent or has resigned from the shop. The new mechanic who gets assigned to it has to take around 4-6 hours to learn every small problem the car has, slowing down the repair shop

### Selection rationale

The current way of orally telling mechanics the quirks of a customer's vehicle has several failure modes. Details of it get warped over time and this misdirection sometimes adds even more time to the repair process because now the mechanic is looking at something that is not related to what the customer suspects needs checking. Another way this fails is that details of the car are sparse. 

## 3\. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

Mechanic’s Notebook and Profiling tool for Regular Customers

### Problem statement

### Proposed solution

A notebook or archive for mechanics to digitally archive certain problems a car has. The details the notebook can contain are mostly things valuable to a mechanic such as what transmission it uses. Vehicle Model, and wear and tear (e.g mileage). There are some special labels repairs shops can attach to a vehicle as well like: COMMUTER, UTILITY, and PROJECT as a shorthand for what is expected of the car. Then a mechanic can file a repair or an issue on a car.

## 5\. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

The users are mechanics, service staff, and owners or managers of small independent repair shops in Iligan. Mechanics and staff write and read notes about a customer’s car when it comes in and after the job, on a computer or phone the shop already has. Owners look up a vehicle’s records. Customers don’t log in this semester. We’ll demo it with fictional cars and customers. 

### In-scope capabilities

1. Users can create profiles for a customer’s vehicle and record recurring problems, past repairs, and mechanic notes.  
2. Users can view a vehicle’s repair history and known quirks so a new mechanic can pick up where the previous one left off without re-learning the car from scratch.  
3. Users can search for a vehicle by plate number or customer name and filter records by date or repair type  
4. Users can add new notes after each job so the profile stays current as the car is being fixed.  
5. Owners can look up a vehicle’s full record to check repair history and assigned mechanics

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. Parts inventory, and appointment scheduling.  
2. Automatic diagnosis or AI-generated notes

### Expected deliverables

1. A working prototype where shop staff can create a vehicle profile, log repair notes, and retrieve a vehicle's history by search.  
2. Documentation of the application's build history, key features, how it works, and how shop staff are supposed to use it.

## 6\. Review and Next Steps

- **Before review**: The system was designed to address the difficulty of diagnosing recurring vehicle problems when experienced mechanics leave or retire. The initial proposal identified the need to store customer information, vehicle details, repair histories, previous diagnoses, and maintenance records. However, some system requirements, data security measures, and procedures for recording and retrieving information still needed further evaluation.  
- **After review**: The system requirements were refined to emphasize accurate recordkeeping, efficient retrieval of previous repair records, data security, and regular data backups. The system will serve as a reference tool that allows mechanics to review previous vehicle problems and repair solutions. The design also considers ease of use, proper data entry, and the preservation of technical knowledge to help new or less experienced mechanics diagnose recurring problems.  
- **Reason**: The changes were made to improve the system's functionality, reliability, and usability. A review helped identify the importance of maintaining complete and accurate repair records, protecting customer information, and ensuring that archived data remains accessible when experienced mechanics are no longer available. These improvements aim to support faster troubleshooting, more consistent repair decisions, and better management of the shop's historical records.

### Biggest remaining risk

The business model of this project is open-core, meaning that the notebook itself is free but businesses can opt in for proprietary plugins custom made by the development team ([https\://dirkriehle.com/publications/2009-selected/the-commercial-open-source-business-model/](https://dirkriehle.com/publications/2009-selected/the-commercial-open-source-business-model/)). The risk is that there may be a low demand for these custom plugins. However, there are other streams of revenue like data migration when shops have to change computers or general IT support and training for this tool
